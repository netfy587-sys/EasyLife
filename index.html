Starting point

The main categories of sport

The easiest way to make sense of sport is by how it's structured. Each category asks something different of the people who play it.

Team sports

Cricket, football, hockey, kabaddi, and basketball all depend on a whole team moving together. Strategy and cooperation matter as much as individual skill.

Individual sports

Badminton, tennis, wrestling, swimming, and athletics come down entirely to one person's preparation, technique, and mental toughness on the day.

Indoor & adventure sports

Chess, carrom, and table tennis test the mind indoors, while rock climbing and rowing test the body and nerve out in the open.

A closer look

The major sports of the world

Every sport carries its own story — where it began, how it's played, and where it stands today.

Team sport · bat and ball

Cricket

Cricket, believed to have its roots in 16th-century England, is India's most followed sport. Each team fields 11 players, and the game is played in three main formats — Test (5 days), One Day International (50 overs), and T20 (20 overs). The side that balances batting, bowling, and fielding best usually comes out on top. India has won the World Cup in 1983, 2007, and 2011.

Team sport · the world's most-watched game

Football

No sport is watched or played more widely than football. Each team has 11 players, and a match runs 90 minutes across two halves; the goal is to get the ball into the opponent's net. Held every four years, the FIFA World Cup is widely regarded as the single biggest event in sport.

Team sport · India's traditional national game

Hockey

India once ruled this sport at the Olympics, winning six straight gold medals between 1928 and 1956. Each side fields 11 players who control the ball with a curved stick. Major Dhyan Chand's stick-work was so exceptional that he became known as "The Wizard of Hockey."

Individual / doubles · racket sport

Badminton

A fast-paced racket sport played singles or doubles, where points are won by sending the shuttlecock over the net and out of reach. Each game runs to 21 points. Players like P. V. Sindhu, Saina Nehwal, and Lakshya Sen have put India firmly on the world stage in this sport.

Team sport · a homegrown Indian tradition

Kabaddi

A sport born entirely out of Indian soil, played without any equipment at all. A "raider" holds their breath, crosses into the opponent's half to tag defenders, and races back to their own side before running out of air. The Pro Kabaddi League has given this ancient game a whole new life on television.

Individual sport · track and field

Athletics

The umbrella term for running, jumping, and throwing events, often considered the oldest branch of organised sport. It spans everything from the 100-metre sprint to the marathon, long jump, high jump, and javelin throw. Neeraj Chopra won India's first Olympic gold in athletics with the javelin at the 2020 Tokyo Games.

Individual sport · water sport

Swimming

Often called one of the most complete forms of exercise, since it engages nearly every muscle in the body. Freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, and butterfly are the four main strokes. At the Olympics, swimming hands out more medals than almost any other sport.

The biggest stage in sport

The story of the Olympic Games

What began on a small field in ancient Greece has grown into the largest sporting event on Earth.

776 BC

The ancient Olympics begin

The first recorded Olympic Games were held in Olympia, Greece, in honour of the god Zeus. Early editions consisted of little more than a single footrace.

1896

The modern Olympics are reborn

Thanks to the efforts of French educator Pierre de Coubertin, the first modern Olympics were held in Athens, Greece, drawing roughly 241 athletes from 14 nations.

1900

Women compete for the first time

At the Paris Olympics, women took part for the first time, competing in events such as tennis and golf.

1924

The Winter Olympics begin

The first Winter Olympics were held in Chamonix, France, featuring snow and ice events such as skiing and skating.

1928

India's golden era begins

The Indian hockey team won its first Olympic gold medal at the Amsterdam Games, launching a period of dominance in the sport that lasted for decades.

Today

A gathering of more than 200 nations

Today, more than 200 countries take part in the Olympics across over 300 events. The five interlocking rings represent the unity of five continents.

More than just a game

Why playing sport matters

Playing sport regularly is widely understood to benefit both the body and the mind.

Physical benefits

  • Strengthens the heart and lungs
  • Builds stronger muscles and bones
  • Helps keep body weight in a healthy range
  • Improves the body's immune response
  • Leads to better quality sleep

Mental & social benefits

  • Reduces stress and anxiety
  • Builds confidence and discipline
  • Develops teamwork and leadership skills
  • Teaches how to lose gracefully and learn from it
  • Builds friendships and a sense of community
Names worth knowing

A few celebrated Indian athletes

Each of these athletes gave India a distinct identity on the world stage, in their own sport.

Cricket

Sachin Tendulkar

The first and, so far, only batter to score 100 international centuries. Often referred to as "the God of Cricket."

Hockey

Major Dhyan Chand

A three-time Olympic gold medallist whose stick-work is still held up as a benchmark in the sport.

Badminton

P. V. Sindhu

The first Indian woman to win two consecutive Olympic medals — a silver and a bronze.

Athletics

Neeraj Chopra

Won gold in the javelin throw at the 2020 Tokyo Olympics, India's first-ever Olympic gold in athletics.

Athletics

Milkha Singh

Known as "The Flying Sikh," he won gold at the 1958 and 1962 Asian Games and inspired a generation of Indian runners.

Boxing

M. C. Mary Kom

A six-time world boxing champion and Olympic bronze medallist who gave women's boxing new visibility in India.

A little detour

Interesting facts about sport

A table tennis ball weighs just 2.7 grams, yet it can travel at speeds over 100 km/h.

The marathon's exact distance (42.195 km) was extended at the 1908 London Olympics so the race would finish in front of the royal family.

A badminton shuttlecock is traditionally made from 16 feathers, usually from a duck or goose.

Chess is considered as much a mental workout as a game, and its traditions are counted among the oldest in organised play.

A football player covers an average of 9 to 12 kilometres over the course of a single match.

The word "kabaddi" is believed to come from the Tamil phrase "kai-pidi," meaning "to hold hands."

The five Olympic rings are blue, yellow, black, green, and red — a combination chosen so that every national flag in the world contains at least one of those colours.

Swimming activates more than 600 muscles in the body at once.

Know the terms

A short glossary of sports terms

Foul
A violation of the rules, for which the referee can award a penalty or other sanction.
Tiebreaker
A special method used to decide a winner when the score is level.
Referee / Umpire
The official responsible for enforcing the rules during a match.
Substitute
A player who comes on to replace another player during a match.
Final
The last and decisive match of a tournament.
Wild card entry
A place in a tournament granted by special permission, without going through normal qualification.
